# Research Report Structure

Use this spine to make a report useful and auditable. Mode references adapt it to the question; sections are options, not a mandatory itinerary.

## Reader-first sequence

1. Conclusion: usefulness, applicability, recommendation and the main limit.
2. Orientation: the concepts, alternatives or relationships needed to understand the decision.
3. Evidence and mechanisms: how the relevant capability works and what sources establish.
4. Capability and limits: conditions, failure modes, operational cost and unsupported use.
5. Evaluation: comparable criteria, trade-offs and concrete next validation.
6. Source index: references, uncertainty and access gaps.

A longer report may split overview, relationships, implementation, capability, limits, evaluation and index into numbered sections. A short report may combine them; no empty or artificial sections.

## Presentation

- Short paragraphs, one main fact or argument per sentence where practical.
- Accurate technical headings with plain-language explanation and terms defined at first use.
- Mermaid for structure, causality, state or sequence; choose flowchart or sequence by the question. No diagram minimum.
- Compact tables for comparisons, decisions and structured facts; avoid excessively wide cells.
- Lists for source indexes and actionable steps. No emoji or promotional language.
- Language follows the reader; preserve original identifiers and technical names.

## Evidence

Cite the actual supporting page or code near the claim, with relevant dates and conditions. Distinguish source fact, inference and proposed recommendation.
For negative claims record what was searched or inspected; not finding something does not prove universal absence.
Collect material unverified claims and access gaps visibly. State how each affects the recommendation and what would close it.

## Artifact and review

Agree report purpose and path before writing. Update an existing owner where appropriate. Provide findings, evidence, recommendation and open decisions for human audit.
The final message states the artifact and important conclusions and limitations, without claiming human approval.
