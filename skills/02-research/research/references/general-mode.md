# General Research

Investigate a technical, conceptual or practical question. Use [report-spine.md](report-spine.md) for reader-oriented presentation, without a fixed section count.

## Frame the question

Identify the reader's decision, required depth, existing material and evidence that could change the conclusion. Resolve consequential ambiguity before searching broadly.

## Gather evidence

1. Start with primary documentation, standards, original research and actual implementations relevant to the question.
2. Read the supporting material, not just search snippets. For implementation claims inspect source and record repository, commit and relevant file.
3. Use community evidence for actual experience and limitations; distinguish firsthand reports from repeated claims.
4. Check freshness of changing facts. Preserve publication, access and version conditions when they affect applicability.
5. Search alternative explanations and contrary evidence for consequential conclusions.

Choose sources by question and reliability, not a universal count. Missing access is a coverage gap, not proof that evidence does not exist.

## Synthesize

Lead with what the reader can use, the recommendation or explanation, and its conditions. Explain relationships and mechanisms only as deeply as needed for the decision.
Use diagrams for causality, structure or sequence and tables for comparable alternatives. Explain terms when introduced; avoid dense unexplained terminology.

## Verify

- Material claims cite actually read sources.
- Source facts, inference, uncertainty and recommendations are distinguishable.
- Negative claims state the checked scope and cannot infer universal absence from a failed search.
- The report answers the agreed question and identifies limitations and useful next validation.
